HiFiBerry DAC+ Pro — Header Replacement

beginner desoldering & passthrough header guide
⚠ Read before you start HiFiBerry explicitly warns this is only for experienced solderers and voids your warranty. Multiple boards have been damaged by people attempting it. Practice on a cheap scrap PCB first — buy any spare board with a through-hole header, desolder it, and get the muscle memory before touching the DAC+ Pro.

Tools you need

Soldering iron — 40W+ with temp control
25W is too weak. You need fast heat or you'll cook the board while waiting for the joint to flow.
Desoldering pump (solder sucker)
Spring-loaded plunger that creates suction. Your primary tool for clearing holes.
Desoldering wick (solder braid)
Copper braid that absorbs solder by capillary action. Backup for stubborn joints.
Flux (paste or liquid)
Helps solder reflow cleanly. Essential for making wick work on oxidized joints.
PCB holder / helping hands
Both hands will be occupied — iron in one, solder sucker in the other.
Isopropyl alcohol (90%+) + brush
Cleans flux residue after you're done. Don't skip this step.
Magnifying loupe or bright lamp
Required for inspecting 40 joints. You can't see a cold joint without good light and magnification.
2×20 stacking / passthrough header
Extra-tall pins that pass through to let another HAT sit on top. Verify pin count before ordering.
Preparation
STEP 01
Practice on a scrap board first
Build the muscle memory before touching the DAC+ Pro.
›

Find any scrap PCB with a through-hole header — an old motherboard, a spare GPIO extender, anything. Desolder it completely. Then solder a fresh header back. The goal is not a working board; it's feeling the timing of heat → trigger until it becomes automatic.

A 40-pin GPIO extension header costs under $2 and is perfect practice material. The motions on that are identical to what you'll do on the DAC+ Pro.
STEP 02
Secure the board — both hands must be free
Use a PCB holder or vise. Do not hold the board with your hand.
›

Clamp the DAC+ Pro in a PCB holder at a comfortable working height. You'll have the iron in one hand and the solder sucker in the other — there is no hand available to hold the board.

If the board shifts while you're working, you risk dragging the iron across the PCB or burning yourself. Secure it before you pick up the iron.
STEP 03
Set iron temperature and tin the tip
380°C for lead-free (factory standard). Tin the tip before starting.
›

Factory solder is almost certainly lead-free, which needs more heat than leaded. Start at 380°C (716°F). A correctly-sized joint should reflow in 2–3 seconds — if it takes longer your iron is too cool and you're damaging the board through sustained heat.

Before touching any joint: wipe the tip on a damp sponge, then apply a small bead of fresh solder to it. A shiny silver tip conducts heat well. A dark, crusty tip doesn't.

Re-tin the tip every 4–5 joints. Oxidation builds quickly and kills heat transfer.
Desoldering the old header
STEP 04
Add fresh solder to every joint first
Counterintuitive but it makes removal much easier.
›

Touch a small amount of fresh solder to each existing joint and let it flow. This mixes with the old solder, lowers its melting point, and breaks up surface oxidation. Every subsequent step will be easier because of this.

Source: Mastering Soldering and Desoldering Header Pins on PCBs — HilElectronic
STEP 05
The solder sucker must be at 90° — no exceptions
Any tilt creates an air gap and kills the vacuum.
›
Hold the solder sucker perpendicular to the board surface. Even a small tilt creates an air gap that breaks the vacuum.
Source: Mastering Soldering and Desoldering Header Pins on PCBs — HilElectronic · Comprehensive Desoldering Guide — Wevolver
STEP 06
Work in a distributed pattern — don't go pin by pin
Skipping around spreads heat and protects the board.
›
Start at corners (1–3), jump to opposite corner (4–6), then fill in between. The goal is no single area gets repeatedly heated.

After every 5–6 joints, pause for 30 seconds and let the board cool. PCB laminate can delaminate from sustained localized heat. Slow and distributed beats fast and linear.

Source: Comprehensive Desoldering Guide — Wevolver
STEP 07
The desoldering motion: heat → position → trigger
The timing is the entire skill. Keep the iron on the joint when you trigger.
›
  1. Pre-load the solder sucker — press the plunger until it clicks.
  2. Touch the iron to the joint where the pin meets the pad. Hold 2–3 seconds until the solder goes shiny and liquid.
  3. Without lifting the iron, bring the solder sucker tip over the joint at 90°.
  4. Trigger. The solder should clear in under a second.
  5. Inspect the hole — it should be open and clear. If solder remains, repeat.
The most common mistake is lifting the iron before triggering. The solder re-solidifies in the fraction of a second before suction hits. Keep the iron on the joint the whole time.
Source: Cleanly unsolder a 2×20 pin header — diyAudio
STEP 08
Use desoldering wick for stubborn joints
Backup method when the sucker doesn't fully clear a hole.
›

Apply a drop of flux to the wick first. Lay the wick flat against the joint. Place the iron on top of the wick — not directly on the joint. The wick conducts heat to the solder and absorbs it by capillary action.

When done, lift the iron and wick together in one straight motion. Don't drag — the wick can stick to a cooling joint and pull the pad with it.

Dry wick on an oxidized joint often does nothing. Always add flux first.
Source: Mastering Soldering and Desoldering Header Pins on PCBs — HilElectronic
STEP 09
Remove the old header — never force it
If it won't pull free, there's solder still in a joint. Find it.
›

With all 40 joints cleared, the header should lift free with a gentle wiggle and minimal force. If it resists, do not pull or lever it. Find which joint is still holding — usually one or two near a corner — and clear it before trying again.

Forcing a stuck header is the number-one cause of lifted pads on Pi HATs. A lifted pad is usually permanent. One joint is holding you back, not the whole header — find it.
Source: HiFiBerry — GPIO passthrough community thread
Soldering the passthrough header
STEP 10
Seat the new header and tack two diagonal corners
Lock alignment before committing to all 40 joints.
›

Drop the stacking header into the cleared holes — it should fall in freely without force. If a pin won't drop through, there's residual solder in that hole. Clear it with wick before proceeding.

Solder one pin in two diagonal corners (e.g. top-left and bottom-right). Check alignment from the side — the header should sit flush and perpendicular. Adjust now if needed; it's easy. Adjusting after 40 joints is not.

STEP 11
Solder all 40 joints — iron touches pin + pad together
Solder feeds from the opposite side of the joint, not onto the iron tip.
›
Touch iron to where pin meets pad. Feed solder to the other side — the solder flows toward the heat. Remove solder first, then iron.
Never melt solder on the iron tip and drip it onto the joint. This creates cold joints and blobs that look fine but aren't electrically reliable.
STEP 12
Inspect every joint with your loupe
Good = concave, shiny. Bad = convex blob, dull, grainy.
›
Left: perfect — concave, shiny. Middle: cold joint — convex and dull, unreliable connection. Right: too much solder, risk of bridging adjacent pins.

Go over every joint in good light with the loupe. Reflow anything that looks dull or convex. Remove bridges with wick. A bad joint won't show up until you're debugging mystery audio problems later.

STEP 13
Clean with IPA, dry, and test before final assembly
Remove flux residue, then confirm audio works before putting anything together.
›

Dip a stiff brush in isopropyl alcohol (90%+) and scrub both sides of the board around the joint area. Flux residue is non-conductive but attracts moisture over time and can cause corrosion. Let it dry completely — a few minutes under light airflow.

Seat the DAC+ Pro on your Pi and test audio before assembling the enclosure. If a joint needs touching up, find that out now.

If audio plays but sounds distorted or has crackling, check for cold joints or solder bridges with the loupe. Both are fixable — just harder to diagnose after assembly.

Risk summary

Risk Likelihood Prevention
Lifted pad from forcing the header High without patience Never force. Find and clear every joint before pulling.
Solder bridge between adjacent pins Medium (40 pins, tight spacing) Use minimal solder per joint. Inspect with loupe. Remove bridges with wick.
Overheated trace from slow or weak iron Medium with wrong iron Use 40W+ iron. Fast heat for 2–3s is safer than lingering low heat.
Cold joint on new header Low if careful Feed solder to the joint, not the iron. Inspect every pin with loupe.
